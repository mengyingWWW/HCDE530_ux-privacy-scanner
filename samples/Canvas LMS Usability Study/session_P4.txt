Interviewer: Show me how you'd reply to a classmate's thread.

Participant: Discussions… find the thread… Reply. Wait, where's the rubric? Our TA said it's attached but I don't see it here — probably buried in Modules next to the week four folder.

Interviewer: How long did that take?

Participant: Three minutes maybe? I'm usually near campus — I meet my study group around Fifteenth and the Ave after class — and we screenshot weird Canvas bugs. Probably shouldn't pass those around, but we do.

Interviewer: Next, complete the peer review labeled Student B.

Participant: Opening peer review… preview pane is tiny. I typed feedback, hit Submit, "submission failed" with no reason. Refreshed, worked second time. I worry the first draft is floating somewhere public — that would be mortifying.

Interviewer: Do you use the mobile app for discussions?

Participant: Sometimes on the bus. Notifications on the app don't match the website — different unread counts. If you need SMS for study scheduling, my number's the two-zero-six one I texted the coordinator.

Interviewer: Anonymity in peer review — any concerns?

Participant: We're supposed to be anonymous, but a profile photo flashed before it blurred. Ambiguous thing: if you know I'm in COM 220 and my thesis-adjacent project is on bilingual radio audiences, that's almost a fingerprint in a small major. Same with pairing my last name and my club officer role.

Interviewer: Overall rating for discussions?

Participant: Five out of ten. Posting is fine; finding rubrics and trusting peer review isn't. Email follow-ups can go to smartinez at uw — same as the consent form.

Interviewer: Thanks, Sofia.

Canvas LMS Usability Study — Session 01: Navigation & Wayfinding

Interviewer: Thanks for making time today. I'm Lisa — I'll ask you to do a few everyday Canvas tasks and think out loud. Everything stays confidential. Sound okay?

Participant: Yeah, totally. Sorry, I should've introduced myself earlier — I'm Daniel. I'm the one who emailed you last week about switching my lab section?

Interviewer: That's right, thanks. Before we jump in, how do you usually open Canvas?

Participant: Um, mostly on my laptop at home. I live out past downtown Bellevue, so I'm usually on the 271 in the mornings if I'm checking on my phone. The phone layout is… honestly kind of a guessing game because the icons don't always have labels.

Interviewer: Got it. First task: find your grade for your recent circuits midterm — EE 331.

Participant: Okay… let me see. Files? No, that's the lab PDF I just pulled. Syllabus — not there. Oh — Grades. Huh. That took me longer than I'd like to admit, maybe four minutes? I always go to Files first because that's where I was just working.

Interviewer: What made Files feel right?

Participant: I literally had the handout open in another tab. My brain was still in "download mode." On the bus I'll tap something, nothing happens, and I just… wait until I'm on Wi‑Fi at the apartment. The building's off Bellevue Way, so the ride's like twenty-five minutes — plenty of time to second-guess every tap.

Interviewer: Next task: open the feedback on Lab 3 and read what your instructor left.

Participant: Assignments — there it is. That was faster, maybe a minute? But I'm never sure if discussion replies are visible to everyone or just the prof. Last quarter someone quoted my post in class and I hadn't realized it was public.

Interviewer: Have you used other LMS platforms?

Participant: Yeah, Blackboard at community college before I transferred here. Canvas feels less predictable. I'm still getting used to which courses pin what.

Interviewer: Anything else about finding your way around?

Participant: I transferred in as a junior in electrical engineering, so some of my modules are labeled with old course numbers from the previous school. That messes me up. And if you need to follow up about the gift card, it's the same uw address I used on the consent form — the one with my initials and twenty twenty-four in it.

Interviewer: Last one — overall confidence finding things?

Participant: Maybe a six on desktop? Three on mobile. I get there, I just don't trust the first click. Oh — and I'm trying to keep my standing up for a grad application, so when a grade posts late I refresh way too much. My advisor Dr. Lindstrom keeps telling me to chill out, but easier said than done.

Interviewer: That's really helpful, Daniel. Thanks.

Participant: Thanks, Lisa.
